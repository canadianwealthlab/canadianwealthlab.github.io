## Quick Answer

The most you can qualify to borrow is not necessarily the most you can comfortably afford. Start with a monthly housing payment that leaves room for taxes, utilities, maintenance, other debts, savings, and an income shock. Then test that budget against the lender’s mortgage qualification rules and your available down payment.

## Start With Your Monthly Budget

Build affordability from cash flow rather than a target listing price. Add the mortgage payment, property taxes, heating and utilities, home insurance, condominium fees where applicable, and a maintenance allowance. Compare the total with reliable after-tax household income.

A sound budget still lets you make retirement contributions, replace a vehicle, manage childcare costs, and absorb irregular expenses. If buying requires every remaining dollar, the purchase price is probably too high even if a lender approves it.

## Understand the Mortgage Stress Test

Federally regulated lenders generally qualify uninsured mortgages using the greater of the contract rate plus two percentage points or 5.25%. This minimum qualifying rate is designed to test whether a borrower could continue making payments after a rate increase or other financial shock.

Passing the stress test is a lending threshold, not a personal recommendation. Lenders also assess income stability, credit, existing obligations, the property, and their own underwriting standards.

## Include Every Upfront Cost

Your down payment is only one part of the cash required to close. Depending on the property and province, buyers may also face:

- Land transfer or property transfer tax.
- Legal, appraisal, inspection, and title insurance costs.
- Mortgage default insurance when the down payment is below 20%.
- Moving costs and immediate repairs or furnishings.
- Adjustments for property taxes, utilities, or condominium charges.

Do not drain the emergency fund to reach a larger down payment. Cash remaining after closing is part of affordability.

## Stress-Test Your Own Plan

Run a downside case before making an offer. Test a higher renewal rate, a temporary income reduction, and a major repair in the same year. For a condominium, allow for fee increases and a possible special assessment.

Also compare the payment at a shorter amortization. A longer amortization may lower the monthly payment but generally increases total interest and can make a stretched purchase look deceptively comfortable.

## A Practical Decision Process

- Set a maximum all-in monthly housing cost from your real budget.
- Subtract estimated taxes, utilities, insurance, fees, and maintenance.
- Use the remainder to estimate a supportable mortgage payment.
- Add your down payment, then subtract closing costs from available cash.
- Confirm the result with the federal [Mortgage Qualifier Tool](https://itools-ioutils.fcac-acfc.gc.ca/MQ-HQ/MQ-EAPH-eng.aspx).
- Seek a preapproval, while treating it as a ceiling rather than a spending target.

Use the [rent vs buy calculator](/calculators/rent-vs-buy) to compare ownership costs with the alternative, not just with your current rent.

## Warning Signs You Are Stretching

- The budget works only if rates fall.
- You would stop all retirement or education saving.
- Closing would leave no emergency reserve.
- Variable income, bonuses, or overtime are treated as guaranteed.
- Maintenance and transaction costs are excluded.
- A small property-tax or condominium-fee increase would create debt.

## FAQ

### How much of my income should go to housing?

There is no universal percentage that fits every household. Debt, taxes, childcare, transportation, pension coverage, and income stability all matter. Use lender ratios as qualification screens and a detailed after-tax budget as the comfort test.

### Is a mortgage preapproval my affordable price?

No. A preapproval estimates what a lender may offer under stated assumptions. It does not know every future goal, irregular expense, or risk in your household.

### Should I buy less than I qualify for?

Often, yes. A lower purchase price can preserve flexibility for repairs, savings, career changes, and higher renewal payments. The right margin depends on the reliability of your income and other obligations.

## Calculate the Full Ownership Budget

Estimate property tax from the municipality, property-specific utilities where available, home insurance, condominium or strata fees, transportation changes, routine maintenance, and a reserve for large replacements.

> Maximum mortgage cash flow = sustainable housing budget minus tax minus utilities minus insurance minus fees minus maintenance provision.

Suppose reliable monthly net income is $9,000 and the household chooses a $3,600 all-in housing limit. Property tax is $500, utilities and insurance $450, maintenance provision $500, and strata fee $350. Only $1,800 remains for the mortgage payment. If the target payment is $2,600, the purchase exceeds the household limit even if a lender approves it.

The maintenance provision is not a monthly invoice. It recognizes that roofs, appliances, plumbing, windows, and building assessments are uneven but real.

## Stress the Renewal

Calculate payment at the expected rate, the federal qualifying rate, and a higher renewal. Federally regulated lenders generally qualify using the higher of 5.25% or contract rate plus two percentage points under current rules. Passing does not prove comfort.

Compare the remaining balance at renewal under the proposed amortization, then apply a rate two percentage points higher. Test lower income and a major repair at the same time.

## Closing and Post-Closing Cash

Keep down payment, transaction costs, immediate repairs, and emergency reserves separate. Legal fees, transfer tax, inspection, appraisal, title insurance, adjustments, moving, and insurance-premium sales tax where applicable can be due around closing.

Using every TFSA dollar may leave no cash for an early repair. HBP withdrawals create future repayment requirements. FHSA rules require qualifying conditions. Verify every program and preserve a post-close balance sheet.

## Income Quality and Debt

Use conservative reliable income for bonuses, commission, overtime, contracts, and self-employment. Lenders apply their own inclusion rules. Include vehicle loans, student loans, credit lines, support, and card obligations. Paying debt before buying can improve cash flow but should not consume the closing reserve.

## Affordability Checklist

1. Set an all-in housing limit from after-tax cash flow.
2. Estimate property-specific non-mortgage costs.
3. Preserve goal contributions and reserves.
4. Calculate payment and balance under several rates and amortizations.
5. Test lower income, repairs, and renewal together.
6. Separate approval from the household decision.
