## Executive Summary

Year-end planning addresses transactions and deadlines that must occur before December 31 or another specific date. Filing assembles slips, calculates the return, and reconciles payments after year-end. Keep them separate. Confirm current deadlines because weekends, holidays, legislation, and program-specific rules can change dates.

This checklist covers ordinary federal and provincial personal tax considerations. It does not cover trusts, emigration, offshore planning, estates, or aggressive strategies.

## Year-End Income and Deduction Review

- Estimate full-year income by source and province or territory of residence.
- Compare tax withheld and instalments with expected liability.
- Review unused RRSP contributions and available deduction room.
- Decide whether an RRSP contribution and deduction fit the plan, recognizing that the first 60 days of the following year have special reporting treatment.
- Confirm FHSA contributions occur within the calendar year for that year's deduction.
- Review eligible employment, business, moving, childcare, support, and other deductions with current rules and records.
- Make eligible charitable gifts by the applicable deadline if giving is already intended.

Do not accelerate spending solely for a deduction. The after-tax cost remains real.

## Investment Review

- Estimate realized capital gains and losses in taxable accounts.
- Check the superficial-loss window, affiliated persons, and identical property before selling for a loss.
- Update adjusted cost base for purchases, reinvested distributions, return of capital, reorganizations, and fees.
- Review foreign income, tax paid, currency conversion, and potential T1135 filing.
- Avoid trades that undermine the investment policy merely for a tax result.

A capital loss can offset capital gains but does not make an uneconomic investment transaction beneficial.

## Registered Account Review

- Reconcile TFSA contributions and withdrawals using personal records.
- Avoid same-year TFSA recontributions without sufficient room.
- Confirm RRSP deduction limit rather than using only the annual maximum.
- Confirm FHSA eligibility, annual room, lifetime room, and participation period.
- Review HBP and Lifelong Learning Plan repayment requirements if applicable.
- Ensure direct transfers use issuer processes rather than unintended withdrawals.

## Self-Employment Review

- Reconcile revenue, expenses, accounts receivable, and business-use support.
- Confirm GST or HST registration, collection, instalment, and filing obligations.
- Estimate personal income tax and both portions of CPP, or QPP in Quebec.
- Review payroll remittances if salary or employees are involved.
- Separate capital assets from current expenses.
- Preserve receipts, invoices, mileage, home-office, and foreign-currency records.

## Filing Preparation

Gather T4, T4A, T5, T3, T5008, pension, RRSP, donation, tuition, benefit, foreign-income, and other relevant slips. Some trust slips arrive later than employment slips. Do not file early merely to receive a refund if material information is missing.

Reconcile slips with records. Report income even when a slip is absent if the law requires it. Review CRA Auto-fill information rather than assuming it is complete or correct.

## Filing Review

Before submitting:

1. Confirm name, address, marital status, dependants, direct deposit, and province or territory.
2. Reconcile every income source.
3. Review deductions, credits, elections, and carryforwards.
4. Check capital gains, losses, ACB, and foreign reporting.
5. Confirm instalments and tax withheld.
6. Compare the result with the prior year and explain material changes.
7. Save the filed return, confirmation, schedules, and supporting records securely.

## After Filing

Review the notice of assessment against the filed return. Investigate changes, update RRSP deduction room, record loss carryforwards, and diarize instalments or future deadlines. A refund or balance owing should be traced to withholding, instalments, income, and claims rather than treated as a performance score.

## Adjustments and Record Retention

If information arrives after filing, use the current CRA adjustment process rather than filing a second return. Keep the original filing, adjustment request, supporting documents, and reassessment together. A changed assessment can affect contribution room, carryforwards, benefits, and instalments in later years.

Retention periods depend on the record and situation. Property cost records may be needed until well after sale, and business records can have specific requirements. Do not delete support merely because the assessment arrived. Verify current CRA retention guidance for the relevant taxpayer and transaction.

## Calendar the Next Tax Year

Record instalment dates, benefit or credit updates, registered-account deadlines, expected slips, and any carryforward decision. For investment accounts, update adjusted cost base during the year rather than reconstructing it only at filing. A short quarterly review reduces year-end errors without turning ordinary tax administration into constant tax trading.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing the RRSP contribution deadline with the calendar-year FHSA deadline.
- Filing before all investment slips arrive.
- Relying on T5008 book value as ACB.
- Missing same-year TFSA recontributions.
- Claiming a superficial loss.
- Ignoring the April 30 balance-due date for self-employed taxpayers.
- Treating a proposed tax measure as enacted.
- Refreshing last year's checklist without verifying current sources.

## FAQ

### Is year-end tax-loss selling always beneficial?

No. The loss must be usable, the trade must fit the investment plan, transaction costs matter, and the superficial-loss rule can deny it.

### Should I file as soon as NETFILE opens?

Only when all material slips and records are available. Filing early with missing information can require an adjustment and delay accurate assessment.

### Do self-employed people pay tax on June 15?

The general filing deadline can be June 15, but balances are generally due April 30. Verify the current year and personal situation.
