## Quick Answer

The Canada Pension Plan retirement pension is a monthly, taxable benefit based mainly on your contribution history, pensionable earnings, and the age you start. You can generally begin from age 60 to 70. The 2026 maximum at age 65 is $1,507.65 per month, but the average for new age-65 beneficiaries in April 2026 was $877.01.

## Who Qualifies

To qualify, you generally must be at least 60 and have made at least one valid CPP contribution. Quebec workers generally participate in the Quebec Pension Plan instead.

One contribution can establish eligibility, but it does not produce the maximum pension. The amount reflects how much and how long you contributed, your earnings, and applicable provisions that can exclude or credit certain periods.

## How Your Payment Is Calculated

Service Canada considers:

- The age when the pension starts.
- The amount and duration of CPP contributions.
- Average earnings through the contributory period.
- Applicable dropout or credit provisions.
- The CPP enhancement for eligible contribution years.

Retrieve a personal estimate through My Service Canada Account. Published maximums are useful context, not a forecast of your payment.

## Choosing a Start Age

Age 65 is the standard reference point, but not a required retirement age. Starting before 65 permanently reduces the monthly payment by 0.6% for each month early, to a 36% reduction at age 60. Starting after 65 increases it by 0.7% per month, to a 42% increase at age 70.

There is no increase for waiting past 70. The best age depends on longevity, health, cash needs, work, taxes, other income, and how delaying affects portfolio withdrawals.

## Working While Receiving CPP

Employment does not reduce the CPP retirement pension. If you work while receiving CPP and are under 70, additional contributions may create a Post-Retirement Benefit.

Contribution requirements can depend on age. Review the current rules if working between 65 and 70, including how an election to stop contributions may apply.

## Tax and Household Planning

CPP retirement benefits are taxable income. Tax is not always automatically withheld at the amount needed for your final return, so plan for instalments or voluntary withholding where appropriate.

Spouses or common-law partners may be able to share a portion of CPP retirement pensions for tax purposes if eligible. Pension sharing does not change the combined total paid and is different from credit splitting after separation or divorce.

## Applying and Checking Your Record

CPP does not always begin automatically. Apply in advance of the desired start date and verify the current process with Service Canada. Review your statement of contributions for missing or incorrect years early enough to investigate.

If applying after 65, limited retroactive payments may be available, but the rules do not make late application a substitute for planning.

## FAQ

### What is the maximum CPP payment in 2026?

The maximum new retirement pension at age 65 is $1,507.65 per month for 2026. Most people receive less because the maximum requires a strong contribution history.

### Can I receive CPP and still work?

Yes. Working does not reduce the retirement pension, and eligible additional contributions can generate a Post-Retirement Benefit.

### Is CPP tax-free?

No. CPP retirement payments are taxable income.
