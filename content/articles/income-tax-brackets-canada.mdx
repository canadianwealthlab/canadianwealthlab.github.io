## Quick Answer

Canada uses progressive tax brackets: each rate applies only to the taxable income within that bracket. For 2026, the federal rates are 14%, 20.5%, 26%, 29%, and 33%. Provincial or territorial tax applies in addition, and credits, deductions, surtaxes, and income type change the final amount.

## 2026 Federal Tax Brackets

- 14% on taxable income from $0 to $58,523.
- 20.5% on the portion from $58,523.01 to $117,045.
- 26% on the portion from $117,045.01 to $181,440.
- 29% on the portion from $181,440.01 to $258,482.
- 33% on the portion above $258,482.

These brackets apply to income earned in 2026 and the return filed in 2027. Thresholds and some tax amounts are indexed and can change each year.

## Marginal Rate Versus Average Rate

Your marginal rate is the tax rate that applies to the next dollar of taxable income, considering both federal and provincial systems. Your average rate is total tax divided by total income.

Moving into a higher bracket does not cause all income to be taxed at the higher rate. Only the portion above the threshold moves into that bracket.

## A Simple Federal Example

For $80,000 of taxable income, the first $58,523 is in the 14% federal bracket and the remaining $21,477 is in the 20.5% bracket. This example is before federal credits and excludes provincial tax.

Taxable income is not always the same as salary. Employment expenses, RRSP deductions, other deductions, and multiple forms of income can change it.

## Provincial and Territorial Tax

Your province or territory of residence on December 31 generally determines the provincial or territorial rates for the year. Each jurisdiction has its own brackets, credits, and in some cases surtaxes or reductions. Quebec administers its own provincial income tax.

There is no single “Canadian tax rate” for a given salary. Two people with the same taxable income can owe different amounts because of province, credits, deductions, benefits, and income type.

## Credits, Deductions and Income Type

A deduction generally reduces taxable income. A non-refundable credit generally reduces tax otherwise payable according to its rules. Refundable credits can produce a payment even when tax is low.

Interest, eligible and non-eligible dividends, capital gains, employment income, business income, and registered-account withdrawals are not all taxed identically. Use a tax return or qualified tax advice for a complete calculation.

## Planning Uses and Limits

Marginal rates can help evaluate an RRSP deduction, charitable donation, bonus timing, or taxable withdrawal. But a rate table alone cannot capture benefit phase-outs, payroll contributions, alternative minimum tax, corporate integration, or household interactions.

Avoid making transactions solely to “drop a bracket.” A deduction saves only a fraction of the amount spent or contributed, and the underlying decision still needs economic value.

## FAQ

### What is the lowest federal tax rate in 2026?

The lowest federal rate is 14% on the first $58,523 of taxable income.

### If I enter a higher bracket, is all my income taxed more?

No. The higher rate applies only to the portion of taxable income inside the higher bracket.

### Do the federal brackets include provincial tax?

No. Provincial or territorial income tax applies separately in addition to federal tax.
