## Executive Summary

Cash supports immediate liquidity. GICs can guarantee principal and rate subject to issuer terms and applicable deposit protection. Bonds can provide income and diversification but fluctuate with interest rates and credit conditions. Match each defensive asset to a date and risk rather than treating them as interchangeable.

A concentrated stock creates company-specific risk that diversification does not reward with a reliable higher expected return. Reducing it can create tax, trading, employment, and timing issues, but retaining it also has an opportunity cost. Build a staged plan based on total household exposure.

## Cash

Cash is appropriate for operating needs, emergencies, and short liabilities. Its risks are inflation, variable rates, tax on interest, and institution exposure. Confirm eligible deposit protection directly.

## GICs

GICs offer a stated term and rate. Review whether they are redeemable, how interest accrues and is taxed, maturity instructions, issuer, and protection category. A ladder spreads maturity dates and reinvestment risk.

A GIC locked beyond the spending date is not a good liability match even if its rate is higher.

## Bonds

Bond prices generally fall when market yields rise and rise when yields fall, with sensitivity affected by duration. Credit risk varies by issuer. A bond fund does not mature as a single bond does, although its holdings continuously roll and its yield adjusts over time.

Shorter high-quality bonds may support near-term stability; longer or lower-quality bonds can be more volatile. Do not classify a high-yield bond fund as a cash substitute.

## Build the Defensive Allocation

Start from required withdrawals and the target portfolio risk. Hold immediate needs in cash, known dates in suitable deposits or bonds, and longer defensive exposure according to the policy. Compare after-fee yield, tax, liquidity, and potential price change.

## Match Risk to the Spending Date

Create a schedule for known withdrawals rather than choosing one product for every defensive need. Money required this year needs immediate access. A known payment several years away may suit a maturity ladder. Long-term portfolio stability can use diversified high-quality bonds, provided temporary price changes are acceptable.

Interest is generally taxed as ordinary income in a taxable account. Registered placement can improve tax efficiency, but account room, withdrawal rules, and the portfolio as a whole matter more than assigning every asset by a single tax rule.

## Review a GIC Ladder

A ladder divides deposits across maturity dates. As each portion matures, it can fund spending or be reinvested at current rates. This reduces dependence on one reinvestment date but does not guarantee that future rates will be attractive.

Record issuer, maturity, renewal instructions, access terms, ownership category, and applicable deposit protection. Automatic renewal can place funds into an unsuitable term or rate if it is not reviewed promptly.

## Measure Stock Concentration

Calculate the position as a percentage of investable assets and total net worth. Add unvested employer awards, pension exposure, salary dependence, and industry-correlated property or business interests.

If one employer provides income and a large equity position, a company setback can reduce both at once.

## Options for Reducing Concentration

- Stop adding and direct new contributions elsewhere.
- Sell immediately and diversify.
- Sell on a written schedule.
- Use tax-aware annual tranches.
- Donate eligible securities when charitable giving is already intended and rules fit.

Each approach trades speed of risk reduction against tax, transaction, and regret risk. Hedging and derivatives add complexity and may be unsuitable; they are outside this platform's scope.

## Tax and Behaviour

An unrealized gain is not a reason to ignore concentration. Estimate tax on a sale and compare it with the downside of continuing exposure. A staged plan can manage tax and behaviour but leaves risk outstanding longer.

Avoid anchoring to the highest past price or waiting to “get back to even.” Base the decision on current exposure and future role.

## Create a Written Concentration Rule

Define the maximum household exposure you are willing to retain, how new employer awards will be handled, and what triggers a sale. A schedule based on dates or portfolio percentages is more controllable than a rule based on predicting the next price.

Include tax in the comparison, but express it in dollars. Paying tax on a gain can still leave more diversified after-tax wealth. When the position is material, coordinate sales with blackout periods, securities rules, compensation plans, and professional tax advice.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating every fixed-income product as principal-stable.
- Buying long bonds for a near-term liability without understanding duration.
- Chasing GIC rates without checking access and protection.
- Excluding employer stock from the household portfolio.
- Letting tax deferral justify unlimited concentration.
- Replacing one concentrated stock with a concentrated sector fund.

## Action Checklist

1. Map cash needs by date.
2. Assign cash, GICs, and bonds to explicit roles.
3. Review duration, credit, access, fees, and tax.
4. Calculate total company and industry concentration.
5. Compare immediate and staged diversification.
6. Obtain tax advice for a material taxable position.
7. Record a sale rule that does not depend on price forecasts.

## FAQ

### Can a bond fund lose money?

Yes. Interest-rate and credit changes can reduce market value. Risk depends on the fund's holdings and duration.

### Are GICs always protected?

No. Eligibility depends on issuer membership, product, ownership category, term, and current protection rules.

### What concentration is too high?

There is no universal percentage. The position's volatility, tax, employment overlap, other assets, and consequences of loss determine urgency.
