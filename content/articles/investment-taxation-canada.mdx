## Executive Summary

In a taxable Canadian account, interest, Canadian dividends, foreign income, and capital gains can receive different treatment. Tax slips help but do not replace records. Investors are responsible for adjusted cost base, foreign-currency conversion, and reporting dispositions. Reinvested distributions can increase adjusted cost base even when no cash is received; return of capital generally reduces it.

For the 2025 tax year, CRA guidance applies a 50% capital-gains inclusion rate. Capital-gains rules have been subject to policy changes and proposals, so verify the enacted rule for the transaction year rather than relying on an older article.

## Interest, Dividends, and Foreign Income

Interest is generally included in income as earned, including interest on many GICs even when cash is paid at maturity. Eligible and non-eligible Canadian dividends use gross-up and tax-credit mechanisms that differ by province. Foreign dividends do not qualify for the Canadian dividend tax credit and are generally reported in Canadian dollars.

The investment with the lowest headline tax rate is not automatically best. Compare expected total return, risk, diversification, fees, and account options.

## Capital Gains and Adjusted Cost Base

> Capital gain or loss = proceeds of disposition minus selling costs minus adjusted cost base of property sold.

Identical properties generally use an average cost across all holdings owned by the taxpayer, potentially across multiple accounts. Brokerage book value may not reflect every adjustment.

Example: buy 100 units for $20 plus $10 commission, then 100 units for $24 plus $10 commission. Total ACB is $4,020 for 200 units, or $20.10 per unit. Selling 50 units uses ACB of $1,005 before selling costs, not the price of a selected purchase lot.

## Reinvested Distributions

A fund may allocate income or gains and reinvest the amount, sometimes through a year-end “phantom distribution.” The taxable allocation can increase ACB even if no cash arrives. Failing to add it can cause the same economic amount to be taxed again through an overstated future capital gain.

Automatic cash distributions used to buy new units also create additional cost base. Keep transaction confirmations and tax slips.

## Return of Capital

Return of capital is generally not immediate income but reduces ACB. If ACB is reduced below zero, additional tax consequences can arise. A large distribution labelled return of capital is not necessarily investment profit; it may be returning part of the investor's own capital.

## Capital Losses and the Superficial-Loss Rule

Net capital losses generally offset taxable capital gains, can be carried back three years, and carried forward indefinitely under current rules. They do not normally offset employment income.

A superficial loss can occur when the investor or an affiliated person acquires the same or identical property in the period beginning 30 days before and ending 30 days after a sale, and the substituted property is still owned 30 days after the sale. The loss is generally denied and may be added to the substituted property's ACB. Spouses, controlled corporations, and some trusts can be affiliated. This is more complex than a simple 30-day waiting slogan.

## Foreign Withholding Tax

Foreign governments may withhold tax from dividends. Treaty rates, asset domicile, fund structure, and account type can affect the amount. A foreign tax credit may be available in a taxable account subject to limits. Foreign tax paid inside a TFSA or RRSP is generally not available for the Canadian foreign tax credit, although treaty treatment may reduce withholding for some directly held assets in some registered accounts.

Do not redesign a diversified portfolio solely around a small withholding difference without measuring added product, currency, trading, and administration cost.

## Foreign Currency

Transactions must be reported in Canadian dollars using acceptable exchange rates. A security can create a Canadian-dollar gain even if its foreign-currency price is unchanged. Track purchase, distribution, and sale amounts consistently.

Specified foreign property reporting can apply when cost amounts exceed relevant thresholds. Registered plans have different treatment. Obtain current tax advice if T1135 reporting may apply.

## Common Mistakes

- Using T5008 box 20 as automatically correct ACB.
- Ignoring holdings of identical property at another institution.
- Failing to add reinvested distributions to ACB.
- Failing to subtract return of capital.
- Claiming a superficial loss without checking affiliated persons.
- Assuming foreign dividends receive the Canadian dividend credit.
- Reporting foreign transactions without currency conversion.
- Letting tax location override suitability and diversification.

## Recordkeeping Checklist

1. Record every purchase, fee, transfer, and sale.
2. Track identical property across accounts owned by the same taxpayer.
3. Reconcile T3, T5, T5008, and foreign slips.
4. Add reinvested distributions and subtract return of capital.
5. Check superficial-loss periods before claiming a loss.
6. Convert foreign amounts consistently.
7. Preserve records until after the final disposition and applicable retention period.

## FAQ

### Does a T5008 calculate my capital gain?

Not necessarily. Reported book value may not equal tax ACB. The investor remains responsible for the calculation.

### Are capital gains always taxed at half the rate of interest?

No. Inclusion rates, marginal rates, timing, losses, credits, and future legislative changes matter. Verify the transaction year.

### Is return of capital tax-free income?

It generally reduces ACB and can increase a future capital gain. It is not automatically economic income.
