## Quick Answer

Paying down a mortgage produces a guaranteed return equal to the interest avoided, adjusted for any relevant tax treatment. Investing may produce a higher long-run return, but it brings volatility, taxes, fees, and behavioural risk. A blended approach is often reasonable when the numbers are close or when certainty has real personal value.

## The Decision

Every extra dollar can reduce debt, enter a registered account, stay liquid, or fund another priority. The correct comparison is risk-adjusted and after tax, not simply “mortgage rate versus average stock return.”

Mortgage prepayment improves a known liability. Investing buys an uncertain asset. The expected return can favour investing while the range of possible outcomes still favours prepayment for a risk-averse household.

## Key Factors

### The guaranteed hurdle rate

A 5% mortgage prepayment avoids interest with certainty. For a principal residence, that benefit is generally not taxable. A non-registered investment may need to earn more than 5% before tax and fees to match it.

### Account room and time horizon

Unused TFSA room improves the investing case because growth and withdrawals can be tax-free. A long horizon makes market volatility easier to tolerate; a short horizon makes the sequence of returns more important.

### Liquidity

Money paid into a mortgage can be difficult or costly to access. Before accelerating payments, protect an emergency reserve and understand the lender’s prepayment privileges.

## Scenario Analysis

### High mortgage rate, modest risk tolerance

Prepayment becomes more compelling as the mortgage rate rises. The guaranteed result can also reduce renewal risk and lower fixed monthly obligations.

### Low mortgage rate, long horizon

Investing may have a stronger expected outcome when the mortgage is inexpensive, registered room is available, and the investor can stay invested through major declines. Expected does not mean guaranteed.

### The balanced option

Splitting surplus cash between investing and prepayment sacrifices the mathematically best outcome in hindsight, but it can improve resilience and make the plan easier to sustain.

## Common Mistakes

- Comparing a guaranteed mortgage rate with a headline historical market average.
- Ignoring investment fees, taxes, and the possibility of poor returns.
- Prepaying while carrying higher-rate consumer debt.
- Leaving no liquid emergency fund.
- Investing aggressively while knowing a downturn would cause panic selling.

## Decision Framework

First, pay expensive consumer debt and establish liquidity. Then calculate the after-tax, after-fee return an investment must earn to beat the mortgage. Consider a range of returns, not one forecast. Finally, choose an allocation you can maintain through both a rate renewal and a market decline.

## FAQ

### Is mortgage prepayment risk-free?

The interest saving is contractually predictable, but prepayment reduces liquidity. Confirm your lender’s terms and keep adequate cash reserves.

### What if my mortgage rate is higher than my expected investment return?

That generally strengthens the case for prepayment, assuming there are no higher-priority debts or liquidity needs.

### Can I do both?

Yes. A fixed split can reduce regret and preserve diversification between debt reduction and financial assets.

## Calculate the Investment Hurdle

For a principal-residence mortgage, interest is generally paid with after-tax dollars. A 5% prepayment therefore provides an approximate 5% after-tax certain saving before penalties and liquidity costs. A taxable interest investment must earn more before tax to match it.

> Required taxable interest return = mortgage rate ÷ (1 - marginal tax rate).

At a 5% mortgage rate and 40% marginal tax rate, the required taxable interest return is about 8.33%. Capital gains and dividends have different tax treatment, but their returns are uncertain. A TFSA investment avoids Canadian tax on qualifying growth and withdrawals, while an RRSP comparison includes the deduction and future withdrawal tax.

## Compare the Same Cash Flow

If $1,000 monthly goes to the mortgage, the balance falls and future interest declines. If it is invested, contributions enter over time rather than receiving the full horizon at once. Use consistent dates. At renewal, a lower balance reduces exposure to higher rates. Investing preserves financial assets, but their value may be lower at the renewal date.

## Scenario 1: High Rate, Short Horizon

Assume a 6% mortgage, five-year horizon, no TFSA room, and a taxable balanced investment. Prepayment has a certain 6% saving and lowers renewal principal. The investment needs to overcome tax and fees over a short period. Prepayment is stronger unless liquidity or another priority dominates.

## Scenario 2: Lower Rate, Long Horizon

Assume a 3% fixed rate, 20-year horizon, available TFSA room, adequate reserves, and capacity for a diversified equity portfolio. Investing has a higher expected result, with a wide range. Prepayment offers certainty and lower obligations. A split can be reasonable.

## Scenario 3: Near Retirement

Assume a 5% mortgage, retirement in three years, and repayment would require a large RRSP withdrawal. The withdrawal can create substantial tax and affect OAS. Compare staged prepayments, ordinary payments, TFSA assets, and the retirement cash-flow burden.

## Risk and Behaviour

Investing while carrying debt is a form of leverage. The plan should survive a 30% market decline without selling or abandoning contributions. If the investor will move to cash after a loss, the expected-return case is overstated.

Prepayment can produce psychological and cash-flow value that is difficult to price. Distinguish preference from arithmetic. Liquidity also has value when income is uncertain or major costs approach.

## Full Decision Checklist

1. Preserve emergency and near-term goal cash.
2. Confirm mortgage rate, privileges, and penalty.
3. Identify investment account and tax treatment.
4. Use after-fee return ranges and consistent timing.
5. Stress a market decline and higher renewal rate together.
6. Estimate tax on assets used for repayment.
7. Consider partial prepayment or a split.
