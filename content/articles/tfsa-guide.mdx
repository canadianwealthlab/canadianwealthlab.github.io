## Quick Answer

A Tax-Free Savings Account is a registered account in which investment growth and qualifying withdrawals are generally tax-free. Contributions are not tax-deductible. The 2026 annual TFSA dollar limit is $7,000, but your personal room depends on age, Canadian residency, prior contributions, withdrawals, and unused room.

## A TFSA Is an Account Type

The name can be misleading: a TFSA can hold cash, GICs, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs, and other qualified investments. The account does not determine the risk. A savings-account TFSA and an all-equity TFSA have very different behaviour.

Choose investments based on the goal and timeline. Money needed soon generally calls for stable, liquid holdings; long-term retirement money may be able to accept more market risk.

## How Contribution Room Works

Canadian residents generally begin accumulating room at age 18, but only for years in which they meet the eligibility rules. Unused room carries forward.

Your available room is broadly:

- Unused room from prior years.
- Plus the current annual limit.
- Plus eligible withdrawals made in the previous calendar year.
- Minus contributions made in the current year.

CRA account information can lag. Keep your own records, especially after contributing to multiple financial institutions.

## Withdrawals and Recontributions

Qualifying TFSA withdrawals are generally tax-free and do not count as taxable income. The withdrawn amount is added back to contribution room on January 1 of the next calendar year, not immediately.

Recontributing in the same year without other available room can create an overcontribution. Excess TFSA amounts are generally subject to a 1% tax per month while the excess remains.

## What a TFSA Is Good For

- Long-term retirement investing.
- A flexible medium-term goal.
- An emergency fund held in suitable low-risk assets.
- Saving when an RRSP deduction is not especially valuable.
- Tax-efficient withdrawals that generally do not increase net income.

The account is flexible, but repeatedly trading speculative investments inside it can create losses and contribution room does not return when an investment falls.

## TFSA Versus RRSP

TFSA contributions use after-tax dollars; RRSP contributions may generate a deduction, but later withdrawals are taxable. A TFSA often deserves more weight when current income is low, flexibility matters, or withdrawals could affect income-tested benefits. An RRSP can be compelling when the deduction is claimed at a higher rate than the eventual withdrawal rate.

## Contribution-Room Example

Assume a resident begins 2026 with $12,000 of unused room, receives the $7,000 annual limit, contributes $10,000, and withdraws $4,000. Remaining 2026 room is $9,000: $12,000 + $7,000 - $10,000. The $4,000 withdrawal does not increase room until January 1, 2027. Contributing another $12,000 in 2026 would create a $3,000 excess if no other room exists.

CRA information can lag institution reports. Reconcile every contribution and withdrawal with personal records, particularly when using several issuers.

## Transfers, Residence, and Investments

Move a TFSA between issuers through a direct transfer. Withdrawing and depositing at the new institution is a new contribution and can create an excess.

Canadian tax residents generally accumulate room from eligible years after turning 18. Non-residents do not accumulate new annual room and can face tax on non-resident contributions, although withdrawals can generally occur. Tax residence is a legal determination, not simply citizenship or mailing address.

A TFSA can hold cash, GICs, bonds, mutual funds, and securities that meet qualified-investment rules. The account does not make a volatile investment safe. Losses do not restore room, while withdrawals restore only the amount withdrawn in the following year.

## TFSA Review Checklist

1. Verify eligibility years and residence.
2. Reconcile room using personal transactions.
3. Use direct issuer transfers.
4. Match investments to the withdrawal horizon.
5. Remove an excess promptly and follow CRA filing requirements.
6. Review foreign withholding and product fees without compromising diversification.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating the CRA portal balance as real-time.
- Recontributing a withdrawal before the next calendar year.
- Forgetting contributions held at another institution.
- Holding investments that do not match the goal’s timeline.
- Assuming “tax-free” means losses are deductible.
- Continuing to contribute while non-resident without checking the rules.

## FAQ

### What is the TFSA limit for 2026?

The annual TFSA dollar limit for 2026 is $7,000. Your personal available room may be higher or lower after carrying forward unused room, subtracting contributions, and adding eligible prior-year withdrawals.

### Do TFSA withdrawals affect government benefits?

TFSA withdrawals are generally not included in taxable income and generally do not affect federal income-tested benefits and credits in the way taxable RRSP withdrawals can.

### Can I have more than one TFSA?

Yes, but the contribution limit applies across all your TFSAs combined. Multiple accounts do not create additional room.
