## Executive Summary

Short-term money should be selected for availability and principal stability before yield. High-interest savings accounts, cashable or laddered GICs, and other high-quality short-term deposits can each fit different dates. Equity investments are generally poor matches for a required near-term amount because they can decline when the money is needed.

Compare access, maturity, rate guarantees, early-redemption terms, tax, account type, fees, and applicable deposit-insurance limits. A higher advertised rate is not better if the funds are locked beyond the goal date or the rate is a short promotion.

## Define the Liability

Record the amount, earliest date needed, latest acceptable date, and whether the amount can change. Emergency cash may be needed today. Property tax has a known date. A home down payment can have a flexible date but a high consequence if the balance falls.

Use separate layers:

- Immediate cash for bills and urgent shocks.
- Stable savings for irregular expenses within a year.
- A deposit ladder for goals with known dates over several years.

## Compare Savings Accounts and GICs

A savings account offers access and a variable rate. A GIC can lock a rate for a term, but access may be restricted or penalized. Cashable GICs trade some yield for flexibility. A ladder spreads maturities, reducing the risk of locking everything at one rate or date.

Example: a $30,000 goal in three years might keep $10,000 liquid and place $10,000 each in one-year and two-year maturities, depending on product terms. As deposits mature, the funds can be reassigned based on the goal. This is a structure example, not a product recommendation.

## Account Tax Treatment

Interest in a taxable account is generally included in income. Interest inside a TFSA is generally tax-free, but using TFSA room for short-term cash can displace higher-return long-term assets. The choice depends on available room, marginal tax rate, goals, and the value of flexibility.

An RRSP is usually a poor general-purpose short-term account because withdrawals are generally taxable and room is not restored. An FHSA can hold stable investments for a near-term qualifying home goal when eligibility and room are verified.

## Understand Deposit Protection

Confirm whether the institution and deposit category qualify for protection from the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation or a provincial deposit insurer, and verify current limits and categories from the insurer. Protection is not based solely on the product using the word “savings.” Securities and funds are different from eligible deposits.

Do not split accounts merely to create a sense of safety without understanding institution membership, ownership categories, and how deposits are aggregated.

## Inflation and Opportunity Cost

Stable cash can lose purchasing power after inflation and tax. That does not make equities appropriate for a short liability. The purpose of short-term money is to be available at the required amount. The lower expected return is the cost of matching the asset to the obligation.

For goals with flexible amount and timing, a mixed approach may be possible. Define in advance how much loss or delay is acceptable.

## Build a Maturity Calendar

Record every promotion expiry, GIC maturity, expected purchase date, and amount needed. Set reminders before automatic renewal windows. A simple calendar prevents a suitable product from becoming unsuitable when a rate changes or funds renew beyond the goal date.

For a goal with staged payments, align deposits with each payment rather than the final date alone. Keep transaction delays and settlement time in mind. Funds that require several business days to reach the operating account are not the same as immediately available cash.

## Review the Plan as the Date Approaches

As uncertainty falls, move from estimated amounts to confirmed obligations. If a home purchase becomes likely, add closing costs and an ownership reserve rather than allocating the entire balance to the down payment. If a goal is cancelled, deliberately reassign the cash to another short-term need, debt, or the long-term investment plan.

## Common Mistakes

- Chasing a promotional rate without noting the expiry.
- Locking emergency cash in a non-redeemable product.
- Investing a required down payment in equities because the average return is higher.
- Ignoring tax on interest when comparing accounts.
- Assuming every cash-like fund has principal guarantees or deposit protection.
- Using RRSP money for a near-term goal without modelling tax and lost room.

## Decision Checklist

1. Define amount and access date.
2. Decide how much must be available immediately.
3. Compare guaranteed rate, variable rate, maturity, and redemption terms.
4. Verify deposit-insurance eligibility directly.
5. Compare taxable and registered placement.
6. Record promotion and maturity dates.
7. Reassess when the goal date moves.

## FAQ

### Is a money-market fund the same as a savings account?

No. Product structures, guarantees, fees, settlement, and protection differ. Review the actual fund or deposit documents.

### Should emergency cash earn the highest available rate?

Rate matters after access and stability requirements are met. The highest rate can be unsuitable if access is delayed or conditional.

### How short is short term?

There is no universal cutoff. The required date, acceptable loss, and flexibility matter. Money needed within several years generally deserves more stability than a decades-long investment.
