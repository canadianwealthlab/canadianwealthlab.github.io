## Quick Answer

Buying tends to become more attractive with a long stay, manageable total ownership costs, and a home price that fits without sacrificing other goals. Renting can be stronger when flexibility matters, the price-to-rent gap is wide, or the renter consistently invests the savings. Compare net worth and lifestyle resilience, not rent versus the mortgage payment alone.

## The Decision

A home is both shelter and a leveraged asset. Buying converts cash into down payment, closing costs, equity, maintenance, and transaction exposure. Renting purchases flexibility and transfers many property risks to a landlord, but does not create home equity.

The result depends heavily on uncertain variables: future prices, interest rates, investment returns, rent growth, maintenance, and how long you stay.

## Key Factors

### Time horizon

Buying and selling have meaningful transaction costs. A longer stay gives ownership more time to absorb those costs. If work, family, or location could change soon, flexibility has measurable value.

### Total carrying cost

Include mortgage interest, property tax, insurance, maintenance, condo fees, and opportunity cost. Principal repayment builds equity and should not be treated as a pure expense.

### The alternative investment

The renter comparison is only credible if the down payment and any ongoing monthly savings are actually invested. Assuming perfect investing discipline can overstate the renter outcome.

## Scenario Analysis

### Expensive market, modest rent

When comparable rent is low relative to the purchase price, renting can preserve capital and reduce concentration in one property and city.

### Long stay and stable finances

Buying may fit when the household expects to stay for many years, can absorb repairs and rate changes, and values control of the home.

### Thin monthly margin

If ownership would consume nearly all available cash flow, the financial and lifestyle risk may outweigh an optimistic appreciation forecast.

## Common Mistakes

- Comparing rent only with the mortgage payment.
- Treating principal repayment as an expense or ignoring it entirely.
- Assuming home prices or stocks will repeat one historical period.
- Forgetting selling costs and major maintenance.
- Buying because of social pressure rather than a durable plan.

## Decision Framework

Compare a genuinely similar home. Estimate the full upfront and monthly ownership cost, model five- and ten-year outcomes, and test conservative assumptions. Then evaluate non-financial priorities: stability, control, mobility, and maintenance responsibility.

## FAQ

### Is renting throwing money away?

No. Rent purchases housing and flexibility. Mortgage interest, property tax, insurance, maintenance, and transaction costs also do not build equity.

### How long should I stay before buying makes sense?

There is no universal break-even period. It depends on transaction costs, price-to-rent ratio, financing, appreciation, and investment returns.

### Should I count home appreciation?

Yes, but use a range of assumptions. A purchase decision that only works with aggressive appreciation may have little margin for error.
