## Executive Summary

A mortgage decision has five connected parts: rate structure, term, amortization, repayment flexibility, and probable life events. Fixed rates provide a known contractual rate for the term. Variable rates move with the lender's prime-rate framework, while payment mechanics differ by product. A longer amortization lowers required payments but generally increases total interest and leaves more principal at renewal. Prepayment privileges can accelerate repayment without permanently increasing the required payment.

Do not choose from a rate forecast. Compare payment capacity, total term interest, balance at renewal, penalties, portability, conversion terms, and the cost of selling or refinancing early. Then stress-test a higher renewal rate and an income disruption. The best mortgage is one the household can carry under plausible conditions, not necessarily the one with the lowest advertised rate today.

## Define Rate, Term, and Amortization

The rate determines contractual interest. The term is the period covered by the current agreement, often shorter than the amortization. The amortization is the schedule used to repay the balance fully if rates and payments followed the assumptions.

Canadian borrowers usually renew several times. A five-year term on a 25-year amortization does not lock the rate for 25 years. The remaining balance is exposed to future renewal rates.

Use lender disclosures for exact calculations because Canadian mortgage conventions, payment frequency, compounding, rounding, fees, and product terms can vary.

## Fixed-Rate Case

A fixed mortgage is stronger when payment certainty protects the household plan, income is variable, a rate increase would force borrowing or goal cuts, or the borrower simply values a known term cost. Certainty has economic value even if a variable rate later proves cheaper.

Fixed can be weaker when a borrower may sell, break, or refinance during the term and the penalty is large. Penalty calculations can differ by lender and product, including interest-rate-differential methods. A low rate does not compensate for unsuitable contract flexibility.

## Variable-Rate Case

A variable mortgage is stronger when the household has room for higher payments, understands the product's adjustment mechanism, and values potential flexibility. It can be weaker when rate increases consume the cash-flow margin or when fixed payments allow principal repayment to slow and amortization to extend.

Confirm whether payments are adjustable or remain fixed, whether trigger rates or trigger points apply, how unpaid interest is treated, and how conversion to fixed works. A conversion right generally uses rates available at conversion, not today's fixed offer.

## Compare Fixed and Variable Properly

Model the same balance, amortization, payment dates, and term. For variable, include several rate paths rather than one forecast. Record:

- Total payments during the term.
- Interest paid.
- Principal remaining at renewal.
- Highest required payment.
- Penalty if the mortgage is broken in each scenario.
- Value of prepayment and portability terms.

If a variable offer begins 0.50 percentage points below fixed, the difference is not a guaranteed saving. Rates can move and the balance changes over time. A break-even spreadsheet should show how long each rate must persist and what the household can tolerate.

## Choose an Amortization

A longer amortization lowers the required payment and can preserve flexibility. It generally increases lifetime interest and slows equity growth. A shorter amortization forces faster repayment but creates a higher fixed obligation.

Compare principal at the end of the term, not just the first payment. A 30-year schedule can look affordable while leaving much more debt to renew than a 20-year schedule.

The choice can be separated from actual repayment. A borrower may select a sustainable required payment and use privileges to accelerate when cash is available. This works only if optional payments occur and are not offset by new debt.

## Understand Prepayment Privileges

Closed mortgages commonly allow some combination of annual lump sums, increased regular payments, and double-up payments. The percentage, timing, reset date, and ability to reduce an increased payment vary.

Confirm in writing:

1. The annual amount and whether it uses original or current principal.
2. The period in which the privilege can be used.
3. Whether unused room carries forward.
4. How payments above the privilege are penalized.
5. Whether payment increases can later be reversed.
6. How the payment is applied and shown on the statement.

Prepayments reduce principal and future interest. Earlier payments generally save more, but should not consume emergency or closing liquidity.

## Term Selection

Short terms expose the borrower to earlier renewal but can reduce the period of break penalties. Longer terms provide rate certainty for longer but can create larger penalties or a premium for that certainty.

Match the term with probable moves, relationship changes, property plans, business needs, and refinancing. Portability can help but has conditions and is not a substitute for evaluating the chance of breaking the mortgage.

## Prepare for Renewal

Begin several months before maturity. Gather the current statement, remaining amortization, property and income documents, tax status, insurance, and any secured line-of-credit details. Test payments at current offers plus one and two percentage points.

Compare the incumbent offer with alternatives on complete terms. Switching may involve qualification, appraisal, legal work, discharge, or registration cost. A straight switch can receive different federal stress-test treatment from a refinance under current rules, but eligibility and lender policy control. Adding funds, extending amortization, or changing borrowers can turn the transaction into a refinance.

Do not wait for the renewal letter if a higher payment would be difficult. More time preserves the ability to compare, repay principal, adjust spending, or consider housing changes.

## Renew, Switch, or Refinance

Renewing is operationally simple. Switching can improve rate or terms. Refinancing can access equity, consolidate debt, or change amortization, but generally requires qualification and can increase lifetime interest.

Consolidating high-rate debt into a mortgage lowers the rate but can extend the repayment period and places the home behind the debt. Calculate total interest and prevent the consumer balances from returning.

## Worked Stress Framework

Assume a $500,000 mortgage, 25-year amortization, and a five-year term. Obtain actual payment schedules at the offered fixed and variable rates. Then model variable increases of one and two percentage points, and renewal at two percentage points above the initial rate.

Add a sale in year three. Request penalty estimates for both products and include discharge or switching fees. Finally, model a $10,000 annual prepayment and compare the balance at renewal. The purpose is to find which variables dominate, not to predict rates.

## Common Mistakes

- Choosing fixed or variable from a confident rate forecast.
- Comparing only initial monthly payments.
- Ignoring balance remaining at renewal.
- Assuming all variable payments work the same way.
- Treating advertised prepayment privileges as identical.
- Selecting a long term without considering a move or refinance.
- Accepting a renewal offer without a complete comparison.
- Extending amortization repeatedly without tracking lifetime interest.
- Using every accessible dollar for a lump sum.

## Decision Framework

1. Set the maximum sustainable payment from household cash flow.
2. Compare fixed and variable terms in writing.
3. Model rate paths and a higher renewal.
4. Compare amortizations using payment and remaining balance.
5. Review penalty, portability, conversion, and prepayment terms.
6. Test probable sale or refinance events.
7. Preserve liquidity and more urgent debt priorities.
8. Start renewal preparation well before maturity.

## FAQ

### Is fixed or variable historically cheaper?

Historical results depend on the starting date and path. They cannot determine the next term or price the household consequences of higher payments.

### Is the shortest amortization always best?

No. It reduces interest only if the payment remains sustainable. A higher required payment that creates expensive borrowing elsewhere is not an improvement.

### Can I make a large payment at renewal?

Often, but timing and lender instructions matter. Confirm the maturity transaction and preserve necessary liquidity.

### Must I pass a stress test to switch lenders?

Current rules can treat eligible straight switches differently from refinancing. Insured status, transaction changes, and lender policy matter. Verify the exact transaction.
